Exercise 4.3

Circle Chords

Original textbook practice rebuilt as visual learning cards. Attempt first; then interact, check hints, reveal the answer and read the worked steps.

1
A circle has diameter 52 cm and a chord of length 20 cm. Find the distance of the chord from the centre.
DRAG DISTANCE — chord changes
The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord.
Use half-chord $10$ and radius $26$.
Answer: 24 cm
1
$d=\sqrt{26^2-10^2}=24$ cm.
✓
Closure: 24 cm
2
A chord of length 30 cm is 8 cm from the centre. Find the radius.
DRAG DISTANCE — chord changes
Half the chord is 15 cm.
Radius is the hypotenuse of the right triangle.
Answer: 17 cm
1
$r=\sqrt{15^2+8^2}=17$ cm.
✓
Closure: 17 cm
3
Two perpendicular diameters meet in a circle of radius $4\sqrt2$ cm. Find the chord joining adjacent endpoints and the two base angles at those endpoints.
DRAG DISTANCE — chord changes
The central angle is $90°$.
The two radii form an isosceles right triangle.
Answer: 8 cm; 45°, 45°
1
Chord $=\sqrt{r^2+r^2}=8$ cm and the remaining two angles are each $45°$.
✓
Closure: 8 cm; 45°, 45°
4
A chord is 12 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 15 cm. Find the chord length.
DRAG DISTANCE — chord changes
Perpendicular from centre bisects chord.
Half-chord $=\sqrt{15^2-12^2}=9$.
Answer: 18 cm
1
Full chord $=18$ cm.
✓
Closure: 18 cm
5
Two parallel chords of lengths 16 cm and 12 cm lie in a circle of radius 10 cm. Determine the distance between the two chords.
DRAG DISTANCE — chord changes
Find each chord’s distance from the centre.
For chord 16: distance $6$; for chord 12: distance $8$.
Answer: 14 cm
1
For chords on opposite sides, total separation is $6+8=14$ cm.
✓
Closure: 14 cm
6
Use the chord-distance theorem in the textbook bridge-arch problem to find the radius of the circle containing the arch.
DRAG DISTANCE — chord changes
Model half the 6 m width and the 2 m rise as a right-triangle/sagitta problem.
Let the radius be $r$; the distance from centre to chord is $r-2$.
Answer: 3.25 m
1
Solve $3^2+(r-2)^2=r^2$ to get $r=3.25$ m.
✓
Closure: 3.25 m
7
In the circle figure with $\angle ABC=120°$, find the requested centre/base angle relation.
DRAG DISTANCE — chord changes
Relate the inscribed angle to the central angle subtending the same arc.
Remember a central angle is twice the inscribed angle on the same arc, accounting for reflex/major arc when needed.
Answer: 30° for the marked base angle in the textbook answer set.
1
Use the circle angle theorem and the isosceles triangle of radii to obtain the marked $30°$ angle.
✓
Closure: 30° for the marked base angle in the textbook answer set.
8
Use the textbook circle/plantation figure to determine the marked angles from chord and centre relationships.
DRAG DISTANCE — chord changes
Equal radii create isosceles triangles.
Translate an inscribed angle into the corresponding central angle.
Answer: $\angle BDC=25°$, $\angle DBA=65°$, $\angle COB=50°$.
1
Angle-chase with the centre and chord theorem gives $25°$, $65°$ and $50°$.
✓
Closure: $\angle BDC=25°$, $\angle DBA=65°$, $\angle COB=50°$.
