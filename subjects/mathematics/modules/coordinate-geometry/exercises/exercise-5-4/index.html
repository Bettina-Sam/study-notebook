Exercise 5.4

Section Formula & Trisection

Original textbook practice rebuilt as visual learning cards. Attempt first; then interact, check hints, reveal the answer and read the worked steps.

1
Find the point dividing $A(4,-3)$ and $B(9,7)$ internally in ratio $3:2$.
MOVE THE DIVISION POINT
Use $P=((mx_2+nx_1)/(m+n),(my_2+ny_1)/(m+n))$.
Here $m=3,n=2$.
Answer: (7,3)
1
$P=((27+8)/5,(21-6)/5)=(7,3)$.
✓
Closure: (7,3)
2
In what ratio does $P(2,-5)$ divide $A(-3,5)$ and $B(4,-9)$?
MOVE THE DIVISION POINT
Let the ratio be $m:n$ and equate one coordinate.
Use the x-coordinate equation first.
Answer: 5:2
1
Solving the section formula gives $m:n=5:2$; y confirms it.
✓
Closure: 5:2
3
Find $P$ on $A(1,2)$ and $B(6,7)$ such that $AP=\frac25AB$.
MOVE THE DIVISION POINT
$AP:PB=2:3$.
Use section formula with $m:n=2:3$.
Answer: (3,4)
1
$P=((2·6+3·1)/5,(2·7+3·2)/5)=(3,4)$.
✓
Closure: (3,4)
4
Find the trisection points of $A(-5,6)$ and $B(4,-3)$.
MOVE THE DIVISION POINT
Trisection ratios are $1:2$ and $2:1$.
Apply section formula twice.
Answer: (-2,3) and (1,0)
1
The two points are $(-2,3)$ and $(1,0)$.
✓
Closure: (-2,3) and (1,0)
5
Use section formula to show $A(7,-5),B(9,-3),C(13,1)$ are collinear.
MOVE THE DIVISION POINT
Check the ratio in which B divides AC.
If both coordinates give the same ratio, B lies on AC.
Answer: They are collinear.
1
Section relations are consistent for x and y, so the three points are collinear.
✓
Closure: They are collinear.
6
A segment $AB$ is extended 25% beyond B. $A(-2,-3),B(2,1)$. Find the new endpoint C.
MOVE THE DIVISION POINT
25% of AB is one quarter of the displacement from A to B.
Vector $AB=(4,4)$, one quarter is $(1,1)$.
Answer: (3,2)
1
$C=B+(1,1)=(3,2)$.
✓
Closure: (3,2)
