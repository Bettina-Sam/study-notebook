Q1Which arrow best shows the position of $\dfrac{11}{3}$ on the number line?
$\dfrac{11}{3} = 3.\overline3$. Drag the marker below to where you think $\dfrac{11}{3}$ sits between $-5$ and $5$.
Divide $11$ by $3$: it's a bit more than $3$, since $3\times3=9$ and $3\times4=12$.
$\dfrac{11}{3}=3.\overline3 \approx 3.33$, which lies just to the right of $3$, closer to $3$ than to $4$.

Q2Find any three rational numbers between $\dfrac{-7}{11}$ and $\dfrac{2}{11}$.
Since both fractions already share denominator 11, the simplest approach is to list the integers strictly between $-7$ and $2$ over $11$.
Any three of $\frac{-6}{11},\frac{-5}{11},\frac{-4}{11},\frac{-3}{11},\frac{-2}{11},\frac{-1}{11},0,\frac{1}{11}$ will do.
For example, $\dfrac{-6}{11}, \dfrac{-5}{11}, \dfrac{-4}{11}$ (any three strictly between $\frac{-7}{11}$ and $\frac{2}{11}$ are correct).
