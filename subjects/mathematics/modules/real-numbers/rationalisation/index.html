2.7 Rationalisation of Surds

Rationalising factor

A rationalising factor is a term that a surd is multiplied (or divided) by to make the whole thing rational.

(i) $\sqrt3$ is a rationalising factor of $\sqrt3$, since $\sqrt3\times\sqrt3=3$, a rational number.
(ii) $\sqrt[7]{5^4}$ is a rationalising factor of $\sqrt[7]{5^3}$, since their product is $\sqrt[7]{5^7}=5$, a rational number.
Thinking Corner
1. In example (i) above, could $\sqrt{12}$ also work as a rationalising factor of $\sqrt3$? Can you think of any other number that would work?

2. Can you think of another rationalising factor for $\sqrt[7]{5^3}$ in example (ii)?

3. If a surd can have many rationalising factors, is there an advantage to picking the smallest one for calculation?
1. Yes — $\sqrt3\times\sqrt{12}=\sqrt{36}=6$, rational, so $\sqrt{12}$ works too (so does $4\sqrt3$, $9\sqrt3$, any $k\sqrt3$ with $k$ a rational multiple giving a perfect square product). There are infinitely many rationalising factors for any surd.
2. Any $\sqrt[7]{5^k}$ where $3+k$ is a multiple of 7 works, e.g. $\sqrt[7]{5^{11}}$ — but that's far messier than $\sqrt[7]{5^4}$.
3. Yes — the smallest rationalising factor keeps the numbers involved as small as possible, which makes the arithmetic that follows dramatically easier.

2.7.1 Conjugate Surds

$\sqrt3+\sqrt2$ and $\sqrt3-\sqrt2$

A binomial surd like $\sqrt3+\sqrt2$ has one rational-looking part and one radical part — its rationalising factor has a special form. Surds like $a+\sqrt b$ and $a-\sqrt b$ (or $\sqrt a+\sqrt b$ and $\sqrt a-\sqrt b$) are called conjugate surds — flip the sign in front of the surd to get the conjugate.

Watch what happens when $\sqrt3+\sqrt2$ meets its conjugate $\sqrt3-\sqrt2$:

1
$(\sqrt3+\sqrt2)(\sqrt3-\sqrt2)$
2
$=(\sqrt3)^2-(\sqrt2)^2$   (difference of squares)
3
$=3-2$
4
$=1$ — rational! The $\sqrt2$/$\sqrt3$ terms cancel completely.

1
(i) Multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{14}$: $\dfrac{7}{\sqrt{14}}=\dfrac{7\times\sqrt{14}}{\sqrt{14}\times\sqrt{14}}=\dfrac{7\sqrt{14}}{14}$
2

3

4
$=\dfrac{25+3+10\sqrt3}{25-3}=\dfrac{28+10\sqrt3}{22}$
5
