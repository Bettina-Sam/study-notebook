2.8 Scientific Notation

Numbers too big
(or too small) to write out

The Sun's diameter is 13,92,000 km and the Earth's is 12,740 km. Comparing those two long strings of digits is a headache. Written as $1.392 \times 10^{6}$ km and $1.274 \times 10^{4}$ km instead, the comparison is instant — the Sun is about $10^{2}$, i.e. 108, times wider than the Earth. That compact form, $N = a \times 10^{n}$ with $1 \le a < 10$, is scientific notation.

2.8.1 Writing a Decimal Number in Scientific Notation

(i) Move the decimal point so there is only one non-zero digit to its left.
(ii) Count the digits between the old and new decimal point — that is 'n', the power of 10.
(iii) Decimal shifted left → n is positive. Decimal shifted right → n is negative.

Expressing a number $N$ in the form $N = a \times 10^{n}$ where $1 \le a < 10$ and $n$ is an integer is called Scientific Notation.

Example 2.28
Express in scientific notation: (i) 9768854   (ii) 0.04567891   (iii) 72006865.48
i
9768854 → decimal point moves 6 places left
= $9.768854 \times 10^{6}$
ii
0.04567891 → decimal point moves 2 places right, so $n=-2$
= $4.567891 \times 10^{-2}$
iii
72006865.48 → decimal point moves 7 places left
= $7.200686548 \times 10^{7}$

2.8.2 Converting Scientific Notation to Decimal Form

(i) Write the decimal number's leading digit(s).
(ii) Move the decimal point by |n| places — right if n is positive, left if n is negative. Add zeros as needed.
(iii) Rewrite the number in decimal form.
Example 2.29
Write in decimal form: (i) $6.34 \times 10^{4}$   (ii) $2.00367 \times 10^{-5}$
i
$6.34 \times 10^{4}$ → move decimal 4 places right (pad with a zero)
= 63400
ii
$2.00367 \times 10^{-5}$ → move decimal 5 places left (pad with zeros)
= 0.0000200367

2.8.3 Arithmetic of Numbers in Scientific Notation

(i) When the powers of 10 are the same, addition/subtraction is easy — just combine the coefficients. (ii) Products and quotients are found using the laws of exponents.

Example 2.30
The mass of the Earth is $5.97 \times 10^{24}$ kg and the mass of the Moon is $0.073 \times 10^{24}$ kg. What is their total mass?
1
Total mass $= 5.97\times10^{24} + 0.073\times10^{24}$ kg
2
$= (5.97 + 0.073) \times 10^{24}$ kg
3
$= 6.043 \times 10^{24}$ kg
Example 2.31
Write in scientific notation: (i) $(50000000)^4$   (ii) $(0.00000005)^3$   (iii) $(300000)^3 \times (2000)^4$   (iv) $(4000000)^3 \div (0.00002)^4$
i
$(50000000)^4 = (5.0\times10^{7})^4 = (5.0)^4\times10^{28} = 625\times10^{28}$
= $6.25 \times 10^{30}$
ii
$(0.00000005)^3 = (5.0\times10^{-8})^3 = (5.0)^3\times10^{-24} = 125\times10^{-24}$
= $1.25 \times 10^{-22}$
iii
$(300000)^3 \times (2000)^4 = (3.0\times10^{5})^3 \times (2.0\times10^{3})^4 = 27\times10^{15} \times 16\times10^{12}$
= $4.32 \times 10^{29}$
iv
$(4000000)^3 \div (0.00002)^4 = \dfrac{(4.0\times10^{6})^3}{(2.0\times10^{-5})^4} = \dfrac{64\times10^{18}}{16\times10^{-20}}$
= $4.0 \times 10^{38}$
Thinking Corner
Don't just read this one — try it. Fill in a coefficient (between 1 and 10) and an exponent for each of two numbers in scientific notation, and check whether your pair actually works before looking at any answer.

1. Two numbers in scientific notation whose product is $2.83 \times 10^{4}$.

× 10^ × × 10^
One valid pair: $1.0 \times 10^{4}$ and $2.83 \times 10^{0}$ (i.e. just 2.83) — their product is $2.83\times10^{4}$. Infinitely many other pairs also work, e.g. $2.83\times10^{2}$ and $1.0\times10^{2}$.

2. Two numbers in scientific notation whose quotient is $2.83 \times 10^{4}$.

× 10^ ÷ × 10^
One valid pair: $5.66 \times 10^{5} \div 2.0 \times 10^{1} = 2.83\times10^{4}$. Infinitely many other pairs also work.
