1.8 Cardinality Applications

Counting people
without double-counting

If you just add n(A) and n(B), anyone in both groups gets counted twice. Subtracting the overlap once fixes it — and that one idea solves a whole family of word problems.

1.8.1 Two-Set Cardinality Calculator

n(A∪B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A∩B)

Drag the sliders — n(A∩B) can never exceed the smaller of n(A) and n(B).

n(A∪B) = 10 + 7 − 3 = 14

1.8.3 Three Sets at Once

n(A∪B∪C) = n(A)+n(B)+n(C)−n(A∩B)−n(B∩C)−n(A∩C)+n(A∩B∩C)

Every pairwise overlap gets subtracted once (it was added twice), and the triple overlap — subtracted three times in that step — gets added back once. Set the count in each of the 7 regions below and watch the diagram and the formula update together.

Try Yourself

Exercise 1.6 · Q4
All students take music or drama or both. 25 take music, 30 take drama, 8 take both. Find (i) only music (ii) only drama (iii) total students.
Only music = n(Music) − n(both). Only drama = n(Drama) − n(both). Total = only music + only drama + both.
1
Only music = 25 − 8 = 17
2
Only drama = 30 − 8 = 22
3
Total = 17 + 22 + 8 = 47
Answer: (i) 17   (ii) 22   (iii) 47 students in total.
Exercise 1.6 · Q5
45 people like tea or coffee or both. 35 like tea, 20 like coffee. Find (i) both (ii) not tea (iii) not coffee.
n(T∪C) = n(T) + n(C) − n(T∩C). Solve for the overlap first.
Both = 35 + 20 − 45 = 10.   Not tea = only coffee = 20 − 10 = 10.   Not coffee = only tea = 35 − 10 = 25.
