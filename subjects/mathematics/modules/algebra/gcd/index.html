Worked Example

Example
Find the GCD of $14xy^2$ and $42xy$.
Concept used: GCD of coefficients, times the lowest power of each shared variable.
1
Coefficients: GCD(14, 42) = 14 (14 divides 42 exactly).
2
Variable $x$: appears as $x^1$ in both → take $x^1$.
3
Variable $y$: appears as $y^2$ and $y^1$ → take the lower power, $y^1$.
4
Final answer
GCD $= 14xy$

Try Yourself

Exercise
Find the GCD of $9a^2b^2c^3$ and $15a^3b^2c$.
Your answer:
GCD(9, 15) = 3.
For each variable, take the smaller exponent: $a$: min(2,3), $b$: min(2,2), $c$: min(3,1).
Answer: $3a^2b^2c$

Greatest Common Divisor (GCD)

The GCD (also HCF) of two or more polynomials is the common factor of the highest possible degree. The idea parallels GCD of integers.

The textbook example compares 14xy² and 42xy: the numerical GCD is 14 and the common variable part is xy, so the GCD is 14xy.

Factorisation method

  1. Resolve each expression completely into factors.
  2. Take factors common to every expression, using the highest power common to all.
  3. Find the GCD of numerical coefficients separately and prefix it.
