Division algorithm for polynomials

The number identity 13=(5×2)+3 motivates the polynomial rule:

p(x)=g(x)q(x)+r(x), where r(x)=0 or degree r < degree g.

p is the dividend, g the divisor, q the quotient and r the remainder. If r=0, the divisor is a factor.

3.6.2 Synthetic Division

Synthetic division is a shortcut that works with coefficients instead of rewriting variables at every step. The textbook’s method: arrange standard form, insert zeros for missing terms, find the zero of the divisor, then multiply-and-add down the rows.

Textbook Example 3.35–3.37
Synthetic division, including a divisor 3x+1 and comparison of quotient coefficients.
The examples show the complete coefficient-table process and then reconstruct p(x)=d(x)q(x)+r.

3.6.3 Factorisation using Synthetic Division

Once one linear factor of a cubic is identified, synthetic division produces a quadratic factor. Useful tests: (x−1) is a factor when the sum of coefficients is 0; (x+1) is a factor when the sum of even-power coefficients (including constant) equals the sum of odd-power coefficients.
