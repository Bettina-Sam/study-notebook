Change the divisor, watch the remainder

Take $P(x) = x^2 + 3x + 2$. Slide $a$ to change the divisor $(x - a)$ — the remainder updates instantly, because it's always just $P(a)$.

02. Factor Theorem

When the remainder is zero

If $P(a) = 0$, then $(x - a)$ divides $P(x)$ exactly — it's a factor, with nothing left over. Try each candidate below on $P(x) = x^3 - 5x^2 - 2x + 24$.

Worked Example

Example
Is $(x + 2)$ a factor of $x^3 - 5x^2 - 2x + 24$?
Concept used: Factor Theorem — a factor of the form $(x - a)$ needs $a = -2$ here, and $P(-2)$ must equal 0.
1
$(x + 2) = (x - (-2))$, so test $a = -2$.
2
Substitute into $P(x)$.
$P(-2) = (-2)^3 - 5(-2)^2 - 2(-2) + 24$
3
Simplify term by term.
$-8 - 20 + 4 + 24$
4
Final answer
$P(-2) = 0$ → $(x+2)$ IS a factor
