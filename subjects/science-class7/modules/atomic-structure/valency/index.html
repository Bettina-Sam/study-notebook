Unit 4 · Atomic Structure 4.4 Valency

How many hands
does an atom have?

Valency is the combining capacity of an atom — a measure of how many hydrogen atoms it can combine with, like how many hands you have to shake with.

01. Read the Valency

Count the hydrogen atoms it holds

TryPick a molecule and see how many hydrogen atoms the central atom combines with.
Chlorine combines with 1 hydrogen atom → valency of chlorine is 1 (monovalent).

Monovalent, divalent, trivalent

Monovalent (valency 1)

e.g. Hydrogen, Sodium.

Divalent (valency 2)

e.g. Oxygen, Beryllium, Calcium.

Trivalent (valency 3)

e.g. Nitrogen, Aluminium.

Variable valency

Some elements show more than one — Iron forms ferrous oxide (valency 2) and ferric oxide (valency 3).
