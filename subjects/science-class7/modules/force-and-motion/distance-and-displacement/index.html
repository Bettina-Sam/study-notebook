Unit 2 · Force and Motion 2.1 Distance & Displacement

How far you walked vs.
how far you ended up

Distance is the total length of the path taken. Displacement is the shortest straight-line distance from where you started to where you finished. They can be equal, or very different.

Key idea

If the rabbit ends up back where it started (after all 4 legs), distance is 12 m but displacement is 0 m — distance and displacement are equal only when the path is a single straight line.

Worked Example

Example
A person travels 10 km along one road to reach point B, but the straight-line distance between the start and B is 5 km. What is the distance, and what is the displacement?
Concept used: distance follows the path taken; displacement is the straight-line shortcut.
1
The path travelled is 10 km long.
Distance = 10 km
2
The straight line from start to B is only 5 km.
Displacement = 5 km
3
Both share the same unit — metre (m) — but distance is always ≥ displacement.

Definitions to remember

Distance

The total length of the path taken by an object to reach one place from another.

Displacement

The shortest distance from the initial to the final position of an object. It has a direction.

Same unit

Both distance and displacement are measured in metres (m).

Nautical mile

Used for aviation and sea transport — 1 nautical mile = 1.852 km. Speed in this unit is the "knot".
