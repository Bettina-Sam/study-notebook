Same trip, two very
different numbers

Speed = Distance / Time. Velocity = Displacement / Time. If you double back on yourself, your speed can be large while your velocity is small — or even negative.

Worked Example

Example
An athlete takes 25 s to complete a 200 m sprint on a straight track. Find her speed and velocity.
Concept used: on a straight track, distance = displacement.
1
Speed = Distance / Time.
$= 200/25 = 8 \text{ m/s}$
2
Velocity = Displacement / Time.
$= 200/25 = 8 \text{ m/s}$
3
Final answer — both are 8 m/s here, since the track is a straight line.

Definitions and units

Speed

Speed = Distance / Time. SI unit: m/s.

Velocity

Velocity = Displacement / Time. SI unit: m/s, with direction.

Uniform vs non-uniform

Uniform speed covers equal distances in equal times; non-uniform speed covers unequal distances.

1 km/h

= 5/18 m/s (since 1 km = 1000 m and 1 h = 3600 s).
