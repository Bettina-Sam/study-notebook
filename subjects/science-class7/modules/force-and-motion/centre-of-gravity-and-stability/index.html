Unit 2 · Force and Motion 2.6–2.7 Centre of Gravity & Stability

Why some things
tip over, and some don't

The centre of gravity (CG) is the single point through which an object's whole weight seems to act. Stability depends on where that point sits relative to the object's base.

01. Tilt It and See

Will it topple?

An object won't topple as long as the vertical line down from its centre of gravity still falls inside its base. Pick a base width and tilt the object to test it.

DragChange the base width, then tilt the object.
Stable — the CG's vertical line still falls inside the base.
Key idea

Stability increases by: lowering the centre of gravity, widening the base, and using a heavy base. This is why racing cars are low and broad, and table lamps have heavy, wide bases.

Three types of equilibrium

Stable equilibrium

Tilted through a big angle without toppling — CG rises when displaced, and returns.

Unstable equilibrium

Topples with the slightest tilt — CG falls when displaced, and does not return.

Neutral equilibrium

Rolls but never topples — CG stays at the same height wherever it's displaced to.

Real-world uses

Bus luggage sits low, racing cars are built low and broad, table lamps have heavy wide bases.
