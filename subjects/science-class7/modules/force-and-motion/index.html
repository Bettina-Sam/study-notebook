Unit 2

Force and Motion

Why does the straight-line path get you there fastest? Why does a merry-go-round rider feel motion even at "constant speed"? This unit builds distance, speed and acceleration from everyday journeys, then uses them to explain why some things topple and others don't.

Learn in this order

Start with distance and displacement — the two are easy to confuse. Use them to build speed and velocity, then acceleration. See all three come alive in motion graphs, then shift gears to centre of gravity and the stability it explains.

Verified against the 2025 Term-1 textbook

Learning Objectives

  • Define distance and displacement, and differentiate between them.
  • Define speed, velocity and acceleration, and differentiate speed and velocity.
  • Draw and explain distance-time and velocity-time graphs.
  • Measure and calculate the speed of moving objects.
  • Know the day-to-day uses of centre of gravity and stability.
