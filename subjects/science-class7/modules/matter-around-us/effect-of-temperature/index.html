Heat doesn't grow
the particles —
it spreads them out

Heating makes particles vibrate more and move further apart, so matter expands. The particles' size never changes — only the space between them.

Key idea

During heating, the mass of matter stays exactly the same — only the volume changes, because the distance between particles increases while the particles themselves stay the same size.

What heating actually does

Solid → Liquid

Melting — particles gain enough energy to overcome the forces holding them in a fixed pattern.

Liquid → Gas

Boiling — particles break free from each other completely and move randomly.

Mass is conserved

The number and size of particles never changes during heating — only the volume, because of expansion.

Real example

An iron lock's particles get slightly further apart in the sun's heat — that's why the latch sticks on hot days.
