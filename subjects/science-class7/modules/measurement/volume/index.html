Unit 1 · Measurement 1.3 Volume

The space a
solid or liquid fills

Volume is the amount of space occupied by a three-dimensional object. Its SI unit is the cubic metre (m³). Regular solids have formulae; liquids are measured directly; irregular solids need a trick.

03. Irregular Solids

Let the water tell you

Drop a stone into a partly filled measuring cylinder. The water level rises exactly by the stone's volume. Note the reading before ($V_1$) and after ($V_2$) — the volume of the stone is $V_2 - V_1$.

Worked Example

Example (Problem 1.3)
Find the volume of a cylinder whose radius is 3 m and height is 7 m. (Take π = 22/7)
Concept used: Volume of cylinder = π × r² × h.
1
Start with the formula.
$V = \pi r^2 h$
2
Substitute r = 3, h = 7, π = 22/7.
$= \frac{22}{7} \times 3 \times 3 \times 7$
3
Simplify.
$= 22 \times 9$
4
Final answer
$= 198 \text{ m}^3$

Try Yourself

Exercise
A cube has a side of 3 cm. Find its volume in cm³.
Your answer:
Volume of a cube = side × side × side.
Answer: 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cm³.
