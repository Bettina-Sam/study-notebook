Unit 1 · Measurement 1.1 Fundamental & Derived Quantities

Every quantity is
built from seven

Mass, length, temperature — some quantities stand completely on their own. Everything else you'll ever measure, from the area of your notebook to the speed of a bus, is just those few combined.

01. Physical Quantities

A value needs a unit

A physical quantity is anything you can measure — mass, distance, temperature, volume. To express it, you need two things: a value and a unit. "I walk 2 kilometres every day" — here 2 is the value and kilometre is the unit. Without the unit, "2" means nothing.

02. Fundamental Quantities

The seven that stand alone

A fundamental quantity cannot be expressed in terms of any other quantity. There are exactly seven in the SI system. Their units are called fundamental units.

03. Derived Quantities

Combine two, get a new one

A derived quantity is built by multiplying or dividing fundamental quantities. Their units — the derived units — are built the same way. Pick two fundamental quantities and an operation below to see what you get.

Length × Length = Area — unit m²
Key idea

Density = Mass ÷ Volume, unit kg/m³. Speed = Distance ÷ Time, unit m/s. Electric charge = Electric current × Time, unit C (coulomb). Every derived unit is built exactly this way — never invented from nothing.
