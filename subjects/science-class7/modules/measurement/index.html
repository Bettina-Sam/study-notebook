Unit 1

Measurement

Everything you measure — a plot of land, a bottle of water, a block of iron, even the distance to a star — comes down to a handful of ideas: what kind of quantity it is, how its unit is built, and how to work it out when the shape refuses to be simple. This unit builds all of that from scratch.

Learn in this order

Start by sorting physical quantities into fundamental and derived. Use that to build up area and then volume — for regular shapes with formulae, and irregular ones with a graph sheet or a measuring cylinder. Combine mass and volume into density, and finally zoom all the way out to the units astronomers use for distances too large for kilometres.

Verified against the 2025 Term-1 textbook

Learning Objectives

  • Identify fundamental and derived physical quantities, and their units.
  • Obtain units for certain derived quantities.
  • Measure the area and volume of regular and irregular shaped objects.
  • Convert the volume of objects from cubic metre to litre and vice versa.
  • Calculate the density of solids and liquids.
  • Define astronomical unit and light year.
