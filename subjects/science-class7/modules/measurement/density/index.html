Density of a substance is the mass of the substance contained in unit volume (1 m³). $D = M / V$. It decides whether an object sinks or floats in a given liquid.

Key idea

Density = Mass / Volume  •  Mass = Density × Volume  •  Volume = Mass / Density. Rearrange the triangle to find whichever quantity you're missing.

Worked Example

Example (Problem 1.4)
A solid cylinder of mass 280 kg has a volume of 4 m³. Find its density.
Concept used: Density = Mass ÷ Volume.
1
Start with the formula.
$D = \dfrac{M}{V}$
2
Substitute M = 280, V = 4.
$= \dfrac{280}{4}$
3
Final answer
$= 70 \text{ kg/m}^3$

Try Yourself

Exercise (Problem 1.5)
A box made of iron has a volume of 125 cm³. Find its mass if the density of iron is 7.8 g/cm³.
Your answer (g):
Mass = Volume × Density.
Answer: 125 × 7.8 = 975 g.

SI unit of density is kg/m³. The CGS unit is g/cm³, and 1 g/cm³ = 10³ kg/m³. Materials denser than a liquid sink in it; materials rarer than the liquid float — this is why a metal coin sinks but a much heavier wooden log floats.
